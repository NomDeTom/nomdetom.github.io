Results

—

Only the equations for the active mode are shown. Equation 9 returns a negative inductance if fed boost conditions, and the datasheet gives no buck-mode output-ripple or IL(DC) equation — in buck, IL(DC) is simply IOUT.

Loop compensation

—

The closed-loop figures rest on two assumptions the datasheet does not make. REA is absent from SLVSGW6 — the 10 MΩ used here is the figure TI give on the E2E forum. And the datasheet gives no expressions for fCOMZ, fCOMP1 or fCOMP2, so the standard type-II mapping is assumed. The component values from equations 24–26 need neither assumption and stand on their own; the loop gain, crossover and phase margin need both.

Equations 25 and 26 are cancellation conditions, not free choices. Substituting them into that mapping makes fCOMZ land exactly on fP and fCOMP2 land exactly on fESRZ — the two pills above check it numerically. What is left is a single dominant pole at fCOMP1 plus the uncancellable right-half-plane zero. Moving C_C or C_P away from equations 25 and 26 breaks that.

REA cancels out of the crossover. Above fCOMP1 the control gain tends to G_EA × R_C × V_REF / V_OUT, with no REA in it. Swept from 1 MΩ to 1 GΩ the DC loop gain moves 60 dB while the loop gain at the designed crossover does not move at all. So REA sets the DC loop gain and the dominant pole — low-frequency regulation accuracy — and has no effect on R_C, C_C or C_P. That is very likely why TI could leave it out, and it is why the crossover and phase margin above are insensitive to it while the DC loop gain is not.

Two different sense resistors. RSENSE = 0.055 Ω is the internal resistor used in equations 19 and 24. RSNS from equation 18 is the external part you fit between ISP and ISN. They are unrelated.

What these parts give you

—

fC never moves the product. Substituting the boost identity (1 − D) = VIN/VOUT into equations 24 and 25 leaves RC × CC = RLOAD × COUT/2, which is the f_COMZ = f_P cancellation restated. Doubling fC doubles RC and halves CC. So pick RC for bandwidth and let CC follow from the product — not from fC.

Snapping RC down is always safe: it lowers fC, moving further inside the §7.2.2.7 bound. Snapping CC is the only step that detunes the cancellation, and it is tolerant — a 5% CC error moves f_COMZ 5%, against a pole a few hundred hertz down and a crossover roughly 30× higher.

Worst case across the range

—

Buck and boost worst cases are computed separately, each from only the operating points in that mode. Points where VIN = VOUT are skipped — neither set of equations applies in buck-boost transition.

Equation 11 loses its efficiency term in the PDF text layer. Extracted as text it reads V_OUT × I_OUT / V_IN, silently dropping the η divisor — which understates inductor DC current by about 10% and undersizes the inductor. η is mandatory, and the datasheet gives no efficiency model, so you supply it.

V_IN(MAX) in equation 9 is not the design's maximum input. TI's claim that 4.7 µH gives “~50% of the highest average inductor current in buck mode” only reproduces if V_IN(MAX) is fed 9 V — the design's operating minimum. The actual 36 V maximum at the same V_OUT = 3.3 V gives 70.9%, and the genuine worst case V_OUT = V_IN(MAX)/2 = 18 V gives 213% of I_OUT. Three defensible readings of one equation span 49% → 71% → 213%, which is why this page takes the operating point as an explicit input and prints it beside every answer.

TI's own cross-reference is wrong. §7.2.2.7 says the boost power stage is modelled by “Equation 18”. It is equation 19 — equation 18 is the unrelated current-sense resistor. The same off-by-one runs through the BQ25798 datasheet, so the number printed beside an equation is authoritative and every in-text citation is suspect.

§6.3.8 never states the units for equation 3. They are RFSW in ohms and fSW in MHz, derived from the datasheet's own three anchors: 49.9 kΩ → 0.3953 MHz against a stated 400 kHz, 100 kΩ → 0.1986 against a 200 kHz floor, and 8.4 kΩ → 2.198 against a 2.2 MHz ceiling. Reading RFSW as kΩ returns 25–28 for every input, matching nothing.

RFSW is the only frequency knob. The part has no frequency input of its own — you fit one resistor on the FSW pin and equation 3 tells you what you got. So a target frequency on this page only ever picks a resistor, and the frequency reported is whatever that resistor actually gives. TI's own worked example is a case in point: 49.9 kΩ is labelled 400 kHz throughout §7.2.2, but equation 3 puts it at 395.3 kHz, 1.2% low.

Constants and where they come from

R_SENSE = 0.055 Ω — §7.2.2.7, the equivalent internal current sense resistor. Not the external R_SNS of equation 18. G_EA = 190 µA/V — §6.5, error amplifier transconductance. V_REF = 1.129 / 0.846 / 0.508 / 0.282 V — §6.5, selected by the VOUT_FS register field. R_EA = 10 MΩ — the error amplifier output resistance, needed for equation 23's DC gain. Absent from the datasheet entirely; this value comes from the TI E2E forum, so it is on weaker footing than the constants above.

What this page does not cover

Buck-mode ripple and I_L(DC) equations are absent by design — in buck, I_L(DC) is I_OUT, and the buck ripple shown here is equation 9 rearranged, marked as derived. Buck-mode compensation: §7.2.2.7 states boost is the restrictive case and gives boost only. Gain margin: with this pole-zero set the loop phase never reaches −180°, so there is no finite gain margin to compare against the > 10 dB target. Phase margin is computed. Recommended 4.7 µH inductors from Table 7-2: Coilcraft XAL7070-472ME (14.3 mΩ, 15.2/10.5 A), Cyntec VCHA085D-4R7MS6 (15.6 mΩ, 16.0/8.8 A), Vishay IHLP4040DZER4R7M01 (16.5 mΩ, 17/9.5 A).

One frame, played as pitch

The chirp's frequency becomes the note. A LoRa symbol sweeps the whole channel, so each one is a rising glide that snaps back at the symbol's value; the preamble is the same glide thirty-odd times, the start-of-frame delimiter runs the other way, and the payload is where the pitch starts to scatter. Nothing about the audio is a measurement, it is the same geometry the trace is drawn from, mapped onto a range you can hear.

    How the frame is built

    A LoRa symbol is one chirp sweeping the whole bandwidth in Tsym = 2^SF / BW. The symbol's value is the point at which the sweep wraps, so a payload reads as diagonals broken at different heights. Four parts go on the air in order:

    • Preamble — unmodulated upchirps. MeshCore sends sf <= 8 ? 32 : 16; Meshtastic sends 16. Both are far more than RadioLib's default of 8, and at a fast spreading factor the preamble can cost more airtime than the payload.
    • Sync word — two modulated upchirps, one per nibble, each nibble times eight. This is the only thing separating two meshes that share a frequency.
    • SFD — two and a quarter downchirps. The reversal is what lets a receiver separate timing error from frequency error: an upchirp moves one way under both, a downchirp moves opposite ways.
    • Header and payload — the header is always 8 symbols at SF−2 bits per symbol whatever low-data-rate optimisation says, which is why the airtime formula carries a lone +8. The payload is ceil(bits / d) × (CR+4) symbols, so airtime is a staircase in payload length rather than a line.

    Airtime is RadioLib's getTimeOnAir() truncated to whole milliseconds, because that is what the firmware itself computes and polices its duty cycle with. Low-data-rate optimisation switches on at a symbol duration of 16 ms, which is a property of 2^SF / BW and not of SF alone: SF11 crosses it at 125 kHz and does not at 250 kHz.

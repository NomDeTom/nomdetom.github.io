Method

Effective capacitance = nominal × (1 + ΔCbias) × (1 + ΔCtemp) ΔCbias is read from the manufacturer's DC bias curve at the applied voltage, measured at 25 °C. ΔCtemp is the temperature curve's change relative to its own 25 °C value, so it adds only the temperature effect and does not double-count the bias loss. Where a manufacturer publishes two temperature curves, one unbiased and one at half rated voltage, this tool blends between them in proportion to the applied bias, up to the biased curve's own voltage. Where only the unbiased curve is published it is used at every bias. ESR, impedance and ripple current come from the same datasheet pages, at 25 °C and no bias. Ripple current is the manufacturer's thermal model in still air, which is optimistic on a dense board. The bank ESR is one part's ESR at the selected frequency divided by the number in parallel, which assumes identical parts sharing the ripple equally and ignores any difference in trace resistance between them. Curves are captured to this page by one script per vendor: scripts/fetch-mlcc-curves.mjs for Samsung, scripts/fetch-murata-curves.mjs for Murata and scripts/fetch-yageo-curves.mjs for YAGEO. Parts whose vendor does not publish machine-readable curves are listed with specs only and marked as having no derating data. Board area is the courtyard around a real land pattern. The pads come from the KiCad official Capacitor_SMD library, whose chip capacitor footprints are IPC-7351 nominal, and the courtyard adds the density level's excess all round: least (C) 0.10, nominal (B) 0.25 and most (A) 0.50 mm. A 0402 is 0.56 × 0.62 mm pads on 0.96 mm centres, so the pad is wider than the 0.50 mm body — an estimate from body size and side extension gets that backwards. A package with no reference pattern falls back to body + 2 × toe by body + 2 × side, with toe and side 0.15/0.01, 0.35/0.03 and 0.55/0.05 mm by density level, and is marked as an estimated land. Body size is the vendor's published dimension where there is one and the nominal package size otherwise, tolerances ignored. It is a planning figure for comparing parts, not a land pattern drawing. Relative Size draws the selected part at the selected quantity to one scale, courtyards touching, which is the closest the parts may legally sit. The pads, body and courtyard are the same figures the board area uses. On the single part tab the curves are the manufacturer's own, as published. On the part bank tab they are worked out for the whole selection at the operating point: total effective µF against bias at the set temperature, total µF against temperature at the set bias, the lines' ESR in parallel against frequency, and the ripple current the bank can carry for a given rise. Picking a line adds that line's own contribution as a second curve. A line with no curve of the kind being plotted is left out of the total, and the note under the chart says so. The bank ESR curve combines the resistive part only, so it ignores the phase difference between unlike parts; near each part's own resonance the real impedance of a mixed bank departs from it. Selected Parts is a bank of mixed parts. A line starts at the quantity that was in the operating point card when it was added, adding a part already on the list adds to its quantity, and + or − on the line changes it afterwards. Clicking a line picks it out in the layout and dims the rest. Effective capacitance adds across the whole selection; ESR combines in parallel, so a line of qty parts contributes ESR ÷ qty and its share of the ripple goes as its conductance. Lines with no ESR curve are left out of the combined figure, which then reads high. Selection Layout shelf-packs those courtyards to one scale; the area total is the sum of the courtyards and ignores the gaps the packing leaves. LCSC codes, library type, stock and price come from the JLCPCB parts library via scripts/fetch-jlc-parts.mjs and are a snapshot from when the page was last built, not live figures.
